2. Data fields that drive fraud, over-invoicing, and favoritism detection

From a financial analyst perspective, the fields below — once normalized and linked (invoice ↔ payment ↔ tender ↔ quote ↔ counterparty) — power business rules, scoring, and ML models.

Invoices & lines

  • Net / gross amounts, currency, VAT rate, issue date, posting date.
  • Supplier identifiers (company reg., tax ID), line descriptions, quantity, unit price, discounts.
  • PO / contract / framework reference tied to the commercial instrument.
  • Billing address vs supplier history (shell-entity risk).

Payments & bank statements

  • Transfer reference, amount, value date, structured narrative (expected invoice ref).
  • Counterparty IBAN / BIC, split payments, many-to-one invoice matching.
  • Gaps: “payment without invoice” or “invoice without payment” inside the audit window.

Public procurement: tenders & quotes

  • Tender ID, opening / closing dates, lots, weighted criteria (price / technical).
  • Per quote: supplier, amount, submission time, price-clustering anomalies.
  • Win history by supplier; enrichment signals (ownership, geography) when available.

Market prices & indices

  • Item codes / taxonomy, price source (catalog, index), reference band.
  • Invoice vs market median % gap — primary over-invoicing signal.

3. Signals to highlight in the PDF export

  • Over-invoicing — price vs market gap; unit-price distribution outliers.
  • Favoritism — abnormal win rate, low rotation of incumbents, “cosmetic” competition.
  • Collusion — low bid spread; synchronized bid levels.
  • Tender manipulation — specifications mirroring a single vendor catalogue.
  • Financial anomalies — duplicate payments, systematic rounding, amounts just below approval thresholds.
